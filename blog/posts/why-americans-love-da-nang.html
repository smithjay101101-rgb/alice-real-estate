Why Do So Many Americans Love Living in Da Nang?

Cheaper than Austin. Closer to the beach than San Diego.

By Alice Pham, Private Rental Advisor in Đà Nẵng · Last updated: 27 September 2026 · 10 min read

Why do so many Americans love living in Da Nang?

Because Da Nang gives Americans the life of a US beach city for a fraction of the price. Rent runs a quarter to a sixth of what my clients paid in Austin, Denver or San Diego, the beach is a public one ten minutes from the airport, crime is low, and people here are genuinely glad to meet Americans.

I have placed American clients in Da Nang since 2019, from retired couples on Social Security to software engineers keeping Pacific time. Their reasons barely change from one year to the next. They want the ocean, a smaller monthly bill, a safe street and a city small enough to learn in a month. Da Nang gives them all four at once.

Americans come to Da Nang for the rent and stay for the beach, the food and the people.

How much cheaper is Da Nang than a US city?

Roughly a third to a quarter of the cost once rent is included. On Numbeo's September 2026 data, living costs including rent are 179 percent higher in Austin, 199 percent higher in Denver and 278 percent higher in San Diego than in Da Nang.

What is it like to live in a beach city this size?

Easy, because the city is built along the beach rather than beside it. My Khe beach runs the length of the east side of Da Nang with free public access, a promenade and palm trees, and the airport sits about ten minutes from the sand. Most of my American clients swim or run on the beach before work at least a few days a week.

Getting here from the US takes one connection. There are no nonstop flights from the United States to Da Nang yet, but the airport has direct routes to Taipei, Seoul, Hong Kong, Singapore and Bangkok. EVA Air and STARLUX both fly from the West Coast to Taipei and on to Da Nang, which makes Los Angeles and San Francisco the simplest places to start.

The weather suits a beach life too. From February to August the city is dry and sunny, and residents spend the rainy months from September to December on indoor work, cafes and trips. The month by month guide covers each part of the year.

A public beach ten minutes from the airport and five minutes from your door is ordinary here.

Will you miss American food in Da Nang?

Not for long, because you can have both. Da Nang's own food is the reason many Americans stop cooking: a bowl of noodles at a local shop costs 30,000 to 50,000 dong, about $1 to $2, and the seafood places along the Son Tra coast sell the morning's catch by weight. When you want a taste of home, the city has proper burgers, pizza and steak.

  • Burger Bros, 30 An Thuong 4. The burger place my American clients send each other to in their first week.
  • Pizza 4P's, 74 Bach Dang, on the Han River. Wood fired pizza with cheese the restaurant makes itself. Book ahead for the river view.
  • El Gaucho and Olivia's Prime Steakhouse. Two of the steakhouses in the luxury restaurant guide, for birthdays and nights when only a ribeye will do.
  • Mì Quảng, bánh xèo and bánh tráng cuốn thịt heo. The Central Vietnamese dishes Americans end up craving when they fly home. The guide to where locals eat tells you where to find each one.

You get a $2 noodle breakfast and a proper burger for dinner, in the same neighbourhood.

Is Da Nang safe for Americans?

Yes. The US State Department rates Vietnam at Level 1, Exercise Normal Precautions, the lowest of its four advisory levels, and Da Nang scores as markedly safer than Austin, San Diego or Denver on Numbeo's crowd sourced crime index.

Violent crime against foreigners is rare here. The State Department's advice for Vietnam is the same common sense you would use in any US downtown: keep your passport in the apartment safe and your phone in your pocket in crowded places.

Vietnamese attitudes toward Americans are among the warmest anywhere. In Pew Research Center's survey, 76 percent of Vietnamese held a favourable view of the United States, rising to 89 percent among adults under 30. My clients feel it daily, from the neighbour who brings over fruit to the coffee shop owner who remembers their order.

Language is solved faster than people expect. Staff in An Thuong cafes, coworking spaces and apartment buildings speak English, Grab shows the fare and route before you book, and I handle the Vietnamese on leases and landlord calls for my clients.

Da Nang is safer than the US cities most Americans move from, and friendlier to them than almost anywhere.

How do Americans get a visa for Da Nang?

Online, through Vietnam's e-visa. US citizens can stay up to 90 days on one, and the official portal at evisa.gov.vn usually approves applications by email in about three working days.

Vietnam e-visa for US citizens, September 2026.
Detail What applies
Length of stayUp to 90 days
Fee$25 single entry, $50 multiple entry
ProcessingAbout 3 working days, longer around holidays
PassportAt least 6 months of validity left
Where to applyevisa.gov.vn, the government portal
  1. Check your passport. It needs at least six months of validity left on the day you arrive.
  2. Prepare two files. A scan of your passport photo page and a passport style photo on a white background.
  3. Fill in the form at evisa.gov.vn. Enter your dates and choose Da Nang International Airport as your port of entry.
  4. Pay by card. Choose multiple entry for $50 if you plan weekends in Bangkok or Seoul during your stay.
  5. Print the approval. Bring it with your passport and hand both to the officer on arrival.

Apply on the government portal rather than a reseller site, which charges more for the same visa. The Bali comparison sets the e-visa against the Indonesian alternative, if you are weighing both.

Five steps, one form and $25 to $50 is all it takes for an American to land in Da Nang.

Can you work US hours from Da Nang?

Yes, and the setup is already in place. Vietnam's median fixed broadband speed reached 307 Mbps in July 2026, seventh in the world on Ookla's index, coworking spaces run fibre, and several stay open late for calls with the US.

What do Americans do on weekends?

They take day trips, because four of Vietnam's best known sights sit within an hour of An Thuong. Hoi An's old town, the Ba Na Hills, the Hai Van Pass and the Marble Mountains are all easy to reach by Grab or motorbike and back in time for dinner.

What is healthcare like for Americans in Da Nang?

Good, with English speaking doctors at two providers that handle most foreign patients. Vinmec Da Nang International Hospital covers emergencies and specialist care, and Family Medical Practice Da Nang, a foreign run clinic in Hai Chau, covers everyday GP visits.

Frequently asked questions

Is Da Nang safe for Americans?

Yes. The US State Department rates Vietnam at Level 1, Exercise Normal Precautions, its lowest advisory level, and Da Nang scores a crime index of 23 on Numbeo against 40 to 48 for San Diego, Austin and Denver. Violent crime against foreigners is rare.

How much does it cost to live in Da Nang as an American?

Numbeo estimates a single person's monthly costs in Da Nang at about $450 before rent. Add a furnished one bedroom in An Thuong at $570 to $840 a month and a single American's total comes to roughly $1,000 to $1,300 a month.

Do Americans need a visa for Vietnam?

Yes. US citizens apply online for Vietnam's e-visa at evisa.gov.vn. It allows stays of up to 90 days, costs $25 for single entry or $50 for multiple entry, and is usually approved in about three working days.

Where do most Americans live in Da Nang?

Most Americans live in An Thuong, the walkable area behind My Khe beach. Others choose the My An beachfront for sea view apartments, Son Tra for quiet streets near the peninsula, or southern Ngu Hanh Son for houses and villas.

Can you fly direct from the US to Da Nang?

Not yet. Flights from the US to Da Nang take one connection, most often through Taipei, Seoul or Hong Kong, and Da Nang International Airport is about ten minutes from the beach districts.

Is the internet in Da Nang good enough for remote work?

Yes. Vietnam's median fixed broadband download speed was 307 Mbps in July 2026, seventh in the world on Ookla's Speedtest Global Index, and Da Nang's coworking spaces run fibre connections.

Do people in Da Nang speak English?

In the areas where Americans live, yes. Staff in An Thuong cafes, coworking spaces, apartment buildings and international clinics speak English, and apps such as Grab show prices and routes in English.
