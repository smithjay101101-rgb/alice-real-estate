What Does a Luxury Rental in Da Nang Actually Get You?

By Alice Pham, Private Rental Advisor in Đà Nẵng · Last updated: 31 August 2026 · 9 min read

Bài viết này hiện chỉ có bản tiếng Anh. Menu và phần điều hướng đã chuyển sang tiếng Việt. Alice nói tiếng Việt và tiếng Anh, nhắn tin cho cô nếu bạn cần giải thích bằng tiếng Việt.

Key takeaways

  • High end rentals in Da Nang run from around 20 million VND a month for a premium one bedroom to over 100 million for a beachfront villa. Most of my clients land between 30 and 60 million.
  • The premium at the top of this market is paid for things that cannot be built more of: an unblocked sea view, a private pool, a low density building, a plot on the sand.
  • Son Tra and Ngu Hanh Son hold most of the villa stock. My An and An Thuong hold the walkable apartments. Hai Chau is the river and city option.
  • Furnishing, management and included services vary enormously between owners in the same building. Ask what is included before you fall in love with the view.

What counts as a luxury rental here?

In Da Nang, a luxury rental usually means a serviced apartment or villa above roughly 20 million VND a month, in a building or compound with professional management, generous space per person, and either a sea, river or long city view. Size alone does not qualify a place. A large apartment facing a wall is a large apartment facing a wall.

The market here is young. Most of the high end stock was built after 2015, which means the buildings are new, the lifts work, and the finishing standard is closer to Bangkok than to Hanoi. It also means there is very little character. If you want an old French villa with a courtyard, that is Hue or Hoi An, not Da Nang.

What the money mostly buys is space and quiet. Da Nang is a loud city at street level. Karaoke carries, construction starts early, and the beach road is busy from six in the morning. Height, setback from the road, and double glazing are the three things my clients notice within a week of moving in, long after they have stopped noticing the marble.

How much does a luxury rental in Da Nang cost per month?

Asking rents at the top of the Da Nang market run from about 20 million VND a month for a premium one bedroom to 300 million or more for a beachfront villa. Two things move these numbers more than anything on the listing. Lease length is the first: twelve months signed up front will usually beat a six month lease by a meaningful margin, and owners of the larger units care about this more than owners of studios do. The second is the season. Prices firm up before the summer domestic tourism months and soften in the wet season, when short stay demand drops and owners start doing arithmetic about empty months.

If you are timing a move and have flexibility, the months either side of the rainy season are when an owner is most likely to meet you on price. The month by month guide covers when that window falls.

Which area suits which kind of tenant?

Four areas hold almost all of the high end rental stock, and they suit genuinely different lives. Son Tra is the quiet, green, view driven option. My An and An Thuong are where you live if you want to walk to coffee. Ngu Hanh Son has the villas and the space. Hai Chau is the city and the river.

Why do two similar apartments rent for very different money?

Because the premium at this level is paid for scarcity, not for square metres. Two three bedroom units in the same tower, same layout, same finish, can sit 30 percent apart on price, and the gap is almost always a view line, a floor, a corner position or a pool.

There are only so many apartments in this city with nothing between them and the water. Once a new tower goes up in front of an older one, that older building loses a feature it cannot get back, and the rents adjust within a season. This is why I am cautious about long leases on sea view units with an empty lot in front of them. Ask what is approved for construction nearby. It is a boring question and it has saved my clients real money.

The same logic applies to private pools, ground floor gardens, corner units with two aspects, and buildings with a low unit count. None of these can be added later. Everything else on a listing, including the furniture, the kitchen and the bathroom fittings, can be bought or upgraded. Pay for the things that are fixed.

What is worth paying for, and what is not?

Worth it: height and view line, distance from the road, a building with real management, and space you will actually use. A serviced building with a maintenance team that answers the phone on a Sunday is worth more than an extra bedroom nobody sleeps in, and it is the single thing clients thank me for later.

Less worth it, in my experience: a huge private pool in a city with public beaches ten minutes away and a rainy season, a fourth bedroom in a two person household, and marketing that leans on brand names. I have shown apartments with famous designer kitchens and a view of the neighbouring building's air conditioning units.

The test I use with clients is simple. If the feature can be bought and installed after you move in, it should not carry a premium in the rent. If it cannot be added to the building at any price, it is the reason the unit costs what it does.

What is actually included in the rent?

Less than most people assume, and it varies between two owners in the same building. Management fee, parking for a car and a motorbike, wifi, water, pool and gym access and cleaning are all negotiated per unit rather than set by the building. Electricity is almost never included, and at the top of the market it is not a small number.

This is the checklist I go through before anyone signs. Get every line written into the contract rather than agreed verbally at the viewing. Asking for it in writing is normal here and no owner will find it awkward.

  • Electricity. Usually billed by the landlord at 4,000 to 6,000 VND per kWh rather than the state rate. Ask for the rate, not just who pays.
  • Water. Often a flat 100,000 to 200,000 VND a month, sometimes metered, occasionally included.
  • Management or service fee. Apartment buildings charge one. Standalone houses do not. Confirm whether it sits inside or on top of the rent.
  • Parking. A car space and a motorbike space are separate line items in most buildings.
  • Pool, gym and facilities. Included in some buildings, a resident fee in others, and occasionally restricted for tenants rather than owners.
  • Cleaning and linen. Standard in serviced apartments, unusual in ordinary leases, and worth negotiating into a villa contract.
  • Wifi. Cheap and usually included, but confirm the speed rather than the fact of it if you work from home.
  • Furnishing inventory. A written list, with anything the owner has agreed to add or replace, and the date it will arrive.
  • Deposit and handover condition. One to two months is normal. Photograph everything on the day you move in.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average rent for a luxury apartment in Da Nang?

Most high end apartments in Da Nang rent between 30 and 60 million VND a month, which is roughly 1,150 to 2,300 USD. One bedroom units in premium buildings start closer to 20 million. Penthouses and beachfront villas run well above this range.

Can foreigners rent luxury property in Da Nang?

Yes. Foreigners can rent residential property in Vietnam without restriction, and a tourist visa is enough to sign a lease. You need a passport, the landlord registers your stay with the local police, and the lease should be written in both English and Vietnamese. Buying is a separate question with real limits, but renting is straightforward.

Which area of Da Nang is best for luxury living?

It depends on whether you want privacy or walkability. Son Tra and Ngu Hanh Son suit people who want space, greenery and views and do not mind driving. My An and An Thuong suit people who want to walk to beaches, cafes and restaurants. Hai Chau suits people who want the river and the city centre.

How long are luxury leases in Da Nang?

Twelve months is standard and gets the best price. Six month leases are widely available at a premium. Under six months you are usually looking at serviced apartments or short stay rates, which are considerably more expensive per month.

How much deposit is normal?

One to two months of rent is normal for long leases, held by the owner and returned at the end subject to condition. Ask for the deposit terms and the handover condition to be written into the contract, with photographs taken on the day you move in.

Are luxury rentals in Da Nang furnished?

Most are, and the standard varies more than you would expect within the same building. Some come fully equipped down to the cutlery. Others have a bed, a sofa and nothing else. Confirm the inventory in writing, and ask what the owner will replace or add before you sign rather than after.

Is electricity included in the rent?

Almost never. Electricity is billed on top of the rent, usually by the landlord at 4,000 to 6,000 VND per kWh rather than the state rate. In a large air conditioned apartment or a villa with a pool pump this is a meaningful monthly cost, so ask for the rate in writing before signing.
